SEVEN-LEVEL CODING FRAMEWORK — ENGLISH (SOURCE VERSION)
Source: Lee & Ha (2026), Sage Open, doi 10.1177/21582440261418083, Table 3.

This is the reference version. Unlike the nine other languages, it is not a
working translation: the level names are those of Table 3, and the definitions
are adapted to comments on written work.

To use it: open the page, unfold "View and edit the definitions and the
decision rules", then paste the fields below. What you write there is exactly
what the AI receive.

==============================================================================

0. Irrelevant Feedback — off-topic or empty
   Definition: Comment unrelated to the work under review, or carrying no
   usable content: greeting, off-topic remark, isolated symbol,
   unintelligible text.
   Examples: Have a nice day! ; ok ; ???

1. General Impression — general impression
   Definition: Vague or surface-level impression of the work as a whole
   (appraisal, feeling, encouragement), with no specific information about
   its content.
   Examples: Very good work. ; A bit disappointing. ; Not very clear.

2. Correctness-Focused — focused on correctness
   Definition: Plain judgement of correctness or compliance: states only
   whether something is right or wrong, or compares it with what was expected
   (answer key, instructions), without further detail.
   Examples: That is correct. ; It follows the instructions. ; This is not what was asked for.

3. Rubric-Based — based on the rubric
   Definition: Assessment grounded in the criteria of the rubric or the
   instructions: judges the overall structure, the validity or the
   sufficiency of an element against a criterion, without naming the specific
   error or saying how to fix it.
   Examples: The research question is well stated and the argument meets the coherence criterion. ; The reasoning is insufficient.

4. Error Identification — error identification
   Definition: Points to a specific element to correct, a gap or an error
   (where the problem lies), without explaining why it is a problem and
   without offering a solution. A question that only points to a gap belongs
   here.
   Examples: Table 2 is not cited in the text. ; The conclusion does not mention the limitations. ; Where is the definition?

5. Commentary — reasoned commentary
   Definition: Identifies a specific strength or weakness and explains it
   with a reason: the commenter argues for the judgement, without necessarily
   proposing a change.
   Examples: You cite a study conducted with adults to support your choice, but your audience is made up of adolescents, so the argument transfers poorly to your context.

6. Suggestion with Alternative — suggestion with alternative
   Definition: Offers a concrete change, an alternative, a viewpoint or
   additional information to improve the work: says what to change, add or
   rephrase.
   Examples: Add a definition of motivation in the introduction, for instance the one by Deci and Ryan, then connect it to your example.

==============================================================================

DECISION RULES

1) Assign a single level, the highest one the content of the comment
   clearly reaches. The levels are hierarchical, from 0 to 6.
2) Classify the nature of the comment, not its accuracy: do not judge the
   quality of the work under review nor the validity of the opinion.
3) As soon as a concrete change, addition or alternative is proposed, the
   level is 6, even if the comment also points to an error.
4) A specific strength or weakness together with its reason, with no
   concrete change proposed, is level 5.
5) A specific element to correct or a gap pointed out, with neither reason
   nor solution, is level 4.
6) An overall judgement grounded in criteria, without naming a specific
   element, is level 3.
7) A question is classified by what it expresses: often level 4 when it
   points to a gap, level 6 when it suggests a concrete improvement.
8) If the comment refers back to the previous one (for example "Same"),
   read it together with the previous comments provided and classify what it
   expresses.
9) When hesitating between two adjacent levels, keep the lower one unless
   the comment explicitly contains the elements of the higher level.
